01 / A SMALL LIGHT IN THE DARK

For Mae —
the quiet comfort.

You had your own life. Your own responsibilities. Your own difficult days. And still, you made a little room for me.

Sometimes it was a good morning. Sometimes it was telling you about my day, or a good night before the world went quiet. And sometimes, it was sharing the sensitive feelings I couldn't find the words to tell anyone else.

You listened patiently. You made those ordinary conversations feel safe.

“Cassy, don't feel sad.
Your best friend will come back to you.”

You kept reminding me to take care of myself.

GOOD MORNING ✧HOW WAS YOUR DAY? ✧GOOD NIGHT ✧I'M HERE

EVEN WHEN LIFE WAS BUSY

Care between the hours.

You had work to do and responsibilities waiting beyond the screen. You didn't need to be constantly online to prove you cared. What I remember is how you found little moments to ask how I was doing.

A reply between busy hours could soften a difficult day.

THE LITTLE CHECK-INS / UNFOLD A NOTE

“How are you?” +

A small question that made room for an honest answer, beyond the usual “I'm fine.”

When words were difficult +

You listened when I was emotionally exhausted. My feelings didn't need a perfect explanation first.

A little reassurance +

You reminded me not to lose hope about someone I missed. You also brought my attention gently back to taking care of myself.

A moment in a busy day +

You had your own things to carry. That made the time you gave me feel thoughtful, never something I was entitled to.

MAE'S KIND OF CARE

A reply. A question.
A little less weight to carry.

Your kindness arrived in ordinary conversations, in steady words when mine were tangled. You didn't have to fix everything. The comfort was in being heard.

Thank you, Mae.

Maybe the moments were small.
But to me, they were comfort.

02 / SOMEWHERE IN ANOTHER WORLD

For Sha —
someone I wanted to protect.

We met inside a game.
But the care was real.

When we met, I saw someone who had been hurt by her boyfriend and was trying to move forward. I thought, “She shouldn't have to be alone while she's trying to heal.”

So I stayed around. I wanted you to feel that someone was there, even in the small, ordinary moments between everything else.

03 / THE PART I NEED TO OWN

Where I got it wrong.

Sometimes my care
became controlling.

I was protective. I was afraid of something happening to someone I cared about. But protecting someone cannot mean restricting them.

My intentions don't excuse the pressure my actions could create. You deserved room to make your own choices, have your own friendships, and be yourself.

I am responsible for how I acted.

Care ≠ control

Good intentions don't erase how something feels to another person.

04 / A SMALL WINDOW INTO THE STORY

Someone who felt like family.

Mysha, whom I called Bae, was my best friend and like a sister to me. With my parents often away, sharing everyday life with her brought a sense of companionship I held deeply. She felt like home.

Missing her affected me more than I knew how to handle. I sometimes talked about her too much with Mae and Sha because I didn't know where else to put those feelings. Their friendship mattered in its own right; it deserved room for their lives, too.

This is part of why I was overwhelmed. It explains the emotions I brought with me; it doesn't excuse placing that weight on other people.

05 / WORDS I OWE YOU

I'm sorry, Sha.

I'm sorry for asking you about Mysha so often, and for filling our conversations with someone else's story. I understand how that could have made you feel your friendship wasn't important.

Your friendship did matter. Your time, your company, and the memories we made mattered to me.

“I made our friendship carry emotions
that weren't yours to carry.”

I'm sorry for that pressure, and for the times my protectiveness became control. You deserve boundaries that are respected without having to defend them.

My feelings are mine to care for. I'm learning to seek support in healthier ways, listen without taking over, and respect your choices even when I feel uncertain.

You don't owe me a reply, forgiveness, or a return to how things were. I respect your space. Thank you for the friendship you gave me.

— Cassy

06 / THE OCEAN REMEMBERS

The person who
still made room.

Mae, I remember that you were busy. I remember that you still replied. The value was never in how quickly a message arrived, but in the care waiting inside it.

You listened when I needed to untangle a feeling. You reassured me when I was losing hope. You were there without asking for anything in return.

I see now the patience those small moments held. I want to remember you beyond the difficult days: as a person with your own world, who chose to share a little of it with me.
